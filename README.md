## Pick the USDT network before you pick a plan

Tether exists on several chains, and the fee difference between them is not marginal on a €2–€20 monthly bill.

| Network | Typical transfer fee | Time to credit | Accepted where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TRC-20 (Tron) | a few cents, typically under $0.50 | about 1 minute (19 blocks) | nearly every host that takes USDT |
| ERC-20 (Ethereum) | $2 to $20+ in gas, depends on congestion | about 3 minutes (12 blocks), longer when busy | nearly every host that takes USDT |
| BEP-20 (BNB Smart Chain) | low, in the same range as Tron | fast | not universal: some hosts accept it, some explicitly refuse it |

The fee figures come from hosts that publish their crediting rules. Host4Fun quotes TRC-20 at under $0.50 and ERC-20 gas at $2–$20+, and notes that gas on a small monthly VPS adds "meaningless cost" to the bill. CheapServ states TRC-20 at "a few cents" across 19 blocks, ERC-20 at 12 blocks with gas on top, and tells customers to choose TRC-20 unless they have a reason not to.

That advice holds for a €2.00 plan more than for anything else. Paying a monthly €2.00 invoice with an ERC-20 transfer means the Ethereum network can collect several times what AVA Hosting does. On TRC-20, the fee is a rounding error.

The catch is coverage. CheapServ generates TRC-20 and ERC-20 addresses only, and says outright that BEP-20, Solana and Polygon transfers are not credited. Host4Fun supports all three of the networks above. So the question "which network is cheapest" has a fixed answer, and "which network can I use" does not. ## The 60-second check that prevents a permanent loss

Cross-chain mistakes in USDT are not recoverable, and two independent hosts describe the outcome in the same terms. Host4Fun: sending ERC-20 USDT to a TRC-20 address "will result in a permanently lost transaction" that "cannot be recovered by Host4Fun or anyone else." CheapServ: a TRC-20 address and an ERC-20 address sit on different chains, and tokens sent to the wrong one "are not accessible to us or to you."

No support ticket fixes this. Run these four checks before you hit send:

1. **Match the address to the network shown on the deposit page**, not to the network you used on your last order. Same-brand addresses can differ per invoice.
2. **Select the network again at your exchange's withdrawal screen.** Exchanges pick a default, and it is not always Tron. If the withdrawal screen says ERC-20 and the deposit page says TRC-20, stop.
3. **Don't reuse an address from another host or an older order.** BEP-20 addresses in particular are easy to confuse with Ethereum-format addresses, and one wrong character sends the funds elsewhere.
4. **Ask before sending if anything is unclear.** Both hosts above say to contact support first when the network choice is uncertain. A two-hour reply delay costs less than a lost payment.

One more thing people miss: the withdrawal fee your exchange charges is separate from the network fee, and some exchanges apply their own identity checks to withdrawals. That is between you and the exchange, and it does not change what the host credits.

## What the sticker price leaves out: currency, top-ups and refunds

**AVA Hosting prices in euros.** USDT tracks the US dollar, so a €5.00 plan does not convert to 5 USDT. The checkout will apply whatever EUR/USD rate is current, which means your Tether amount moves with the currency market even though Tether itself doesn't. Hosts that price in dollars avoid this entirely: at Host4Fun, a $14 invoice is 14 USDT, full stop. Budget a little slack in your wallet for the euro conversion rather than sending the exact euro figure in dollars.

**Minimum top-ups vary wildly.** CheapServ's minimum is **$25**, credited to a USD balance that never expires, and unused balance is refundable in USDT on the same network you paid from, minus the network fee, within 14 days of the top-up. If you're buying a €2.00 plan, a $25 top-up means carrying credit you may not spend for a year. AVA Hosting's plan pages don't publish a minimum top-up; its smallest listed plan is €2.00.

For AVA Hosting, one third-party review mentions a 30-day money-back guarantee, and the Self-hosted Start page carries a **Free Trial** badge. Neither is spelled out on the plan pages themselves, so confirm the trial length and refund conditions with support before paying for a month you might not need.
